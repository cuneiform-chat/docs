# Commerce

**Connect your store and your agents stop guessing.** Instead of answering from a document somebody remembered to update last quarter, they read your real catalog, your real stock levels, and your real orders — at the moment the shopper asks.

Cuneiform Chat connects to two commerce platforms, and treats them as peers:

- **[Shopify](/commerce/shopify)** — installed from the Shopify App Store and run as an embedded app inside your Shopify admin.
- **[WooCommerce](/commerce/woocommerce)** — connected to your self-hosted store over the WooCommerce REST API.

## Two platforms, one set of features

Shopify and WooCommerce are peers here — not a primary and a fallback. Every capability in the table above works on both. Where behavior genuinely differs, it is because the underlying platform differs, and each difference is called out on the page that covers it:

| Difference | Shopify | WooCommerce |
|---|---|---|
| **How you connect** | Install from the Shopify App Store, then link your workspace from inside the embedded app | Enter your store URL and approve a one-time authorization, or paste API keys manually |
| **Re-authorizing later** | No reconnect step — permission changes ride the app itself | **Reconnect** re-runs the approval on your store |
| **Initial catalog sync** | Starts automatically at connect | Starts automatically on the guided flow; the manual key path needs a **Re-sync now** — see [WooCommerce](/commerce/woocommerce#after-a-manual-connect) |
| **Inventory freshness** | Dedicated inventory updates, so a stock-only change arrives on its own, usually within seconds | Stock changes arrive with product updates, so a stock-only change waits for the next product edit or reconcile |
| **Checkout handoff** | One cart link covering every line | Depends on the permission your keys carry — see [Checkout](/commerce/checkout) |

## You can connect both at once [#you-can-connect-both-at-once]

One store per platform, per workspace — and **a Shopify store and a WooCommerce store can be connected at the same time**. That is the multi-store setup a merchant actually sees: not five Shopify stores, but a Shopify store and a Woo store side by side.

When both are connected, **each store gets its own store agent**, and each agent answers from its own store. A shopper talking to your Shopify store agent gets Shopify products and Shopify orders; the WooCommerce agent answers from WooCommerce. The two are not merged into one combined catalog. Analytics keeps them separate too — each store has its own Commerce Analytics page, under its own entry in the Commerce menu.

<Callout type="warning">
  A store can only be connected to one workspace at a time. If someone already connected the same store elsewhere, the connect attempt is refused with a message telling you to disconnect it there first.
</Callout>

## Your store agent [#your-store-agent]

Commerce is not switched on for every agent at once. When you connect a store, **one agent becomes your store agent** — and it is the agent that carries the commerce tools and reads your catalog.

That agent is chosen for you, not by you. If you have connected this same store before, its previous store agent is reclaimed with its tuning and version history intact; otherwise a fresh one is created. You do not manage this with a per-agent switch: the store agent is shown on the agent's own page as a read-only "managed by your store" panel, so it is always obvious which agent is wired to which store.

<Callout type="info">
  If your workspace has already used up its allowance of agents, there is no room to create a store agent. The connect flow tells you so and asks you to **upgrade your plan, or archive an unused agent and reconnect your store** to finish setup. This is the one place your plan's limits touch commerce, and it is about agent count, not about commerce itself.
</Callout>

## Getting started

1. **Pick your platform.** Follow [Shopify](/commerce/shopify) or [WooCommerce](/commerce/woocommerce) — the connect flows are quite different, the results are the same.
2. **Let the catalog sync.** The first import pulls your products, variants, prices, images, and stock. See [Catalog & Knowledge](/commerce/catalog-and-knowledge).
3. **Ask your store agent a catalog question.** "What sizes does the linen shirt come in?" is the fastest proof that it worked.
4. **Turn on what you want.** [Order-status updates](/commerce/orders#order-status-updates), [shoppable chat](/commerce/shoppable-chat), a [Product Finder quiz](/commerce/product-tools#product-finder) — each is independent.
